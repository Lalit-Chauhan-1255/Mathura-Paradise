ABOUT MATHURA

The Sacred City of Mathura

Mathura is one of the most ancient and culturally significant cities of India. Situated on the banks of the Yamuna River, the city is widely known for its deep connection with Lord Krishna and the traditions of Braj.

Mathura is surrounded by a rich spiritual landscape of temples, ghats, historic sites and traditional settlements. Together with Vrindavan and other destinations of Braj, it forms an important centre of pilgrimage, culture and tourism.

The city attracts visitors throughout the year who come to experience its temples, festivals, devotional traditions, local culture, architecture and peaceful atmosphere along the Yamuna.

A JOURNEY THROUGH TIME

History of Mathura

A city where ancient history, spirituality and living traditions come together.

01

Ancient Mathura

Mathura is one of India's ancient cities, known for its rich history and cultural significance. Located on the banks of the Yamuna River, it developed as an important centre of trade, art, religion and civilization. Its archaeological remains and historic landmarks reflect centuries of Indian history and the city's enduring cultural importance.

02

Krishna Tradition

Mathura is traditionally regarded as the birthplace of Lord Krishna and holds great importance in Hindu tradition. The city is closely connected with stories of Krishna's birth and divine life. Its sacred temples, religious festivals, devotional music and pilgrimage sites attract devotees throughout the year, especially during Janmashtami.

03

Cultural Heritage

Mathura has a rich cultural heritage reflected in its ancient sculptures, traditional architecture, religious customs and vibrant festivals. The region is an important centre of Braj culture, known for devotional music, folk traditions, colourful celebrations and distinctive artistic styles.

04

Mathura Today

Today, Mathura is a major religious and tourist destination, welcoming pilgrims and travellers from across India and abroad. Its temples, sacred ghats, bustling markets and nearby Vrindavan offer a blend of spirituality, history and local culture. The city continues to preserve its traditions while welcoming visitors to Braj Bhoomi.
